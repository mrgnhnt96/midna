# Changelog

Notable changes to midna for users. Each release gets a section here, newest first. The website shows this file at [midna.mrgnhnt.com/changelog](https://midna.mrgnhnt.com/changelog/).

## Unreleased

The first public release.

### New

- **A terminal agents can drive.** Claude Code, Codex and any other tool can open terminals, send input, read screens and raise your attention through the `midna` CLI or its MCP server. `midna capabilities` lists what an agent can do and what only you can.
- **Shells that survive everything.** A background daemon owns every terminal. Quit midna, update it or restart the daemon, and your shells keep running with the same processes and screens.
- **Needs you.** Permission prompts, failed monitors and agent requests from every terminal wait in one queue. Press <kbd>⌘J</kbd> to go through them, and approve once, for a while, for the session or always.
- **Rules.** Allow, ask or deny commands and tools, globally or per project. Agents can add rules; only you can remove them.
- **Triggers.** GitHub and Bitbucket webhooks can start an agent, run a command or raise your attention, delivered over Tailscale Funnel.
- **Insights.** Turns, messages, spend and time spent waiting on you, by day, week or month.
- **Keep the Mac awake for agents.** Turn on **Settings › Agents › Keep the Mac awake** (or `midna keep-awake on`) and midna stops your Mac from idle-sleeping during your work hours while agents have work: one working, a queued message, a resume, a scheduled trigger. Drag the hours across a 24-hour bar, tap the days, give single days their own hours with `midna keep-awake day`, turn it off for today or on for a while (`midna keep-awake for 5h`, or `until 1am` past midnight; your hours take over after), and it lets go on low battery. Only idle sleep: the display still sleeps and closing the lid still sleeps the Mac. Tools read and change it through `keep_awake.status` / `keep_awake.set`.
- **Wake the Mac for scheduled work.** Turn on **Settings › Agents › Wake the Mac for scheduled work** (or `midna keep-awake wake setup`) and midna wakes a sleeping Mac 2 minutes before the next work due inside your keep-awake hours (a schedule trigger, a message queued for a time, an agent's wakeup) and keeps it awake until that work runs, so a 7 AM trigger starts while the screen is still locked. It asks for your password once to install a helper that can only schedule midna's own wakes; `midna keep-awake wake remove` takes it out. Leave the lid open.
- **Updates wait for a calm Mac.** When your Mac is very busy, midna holds off moving its background service to a new version, because under heavy load that handoff can time out and close your terminals. A window says so once and lets you **Wait** or **Update anyway**; midna updates by itself when the load drops. `midna daemon upgrade|restart --force` goes ahead regardless; **Settings › Agents › Runaway work** sets what counts as busy.
- **Pause or stop the terminal hogging your Mac.** When the Mac stays overloaded for a couple of minutes, midna lists the terminals using the most CPU (`rustc ×14`) with **Pause**, **Resume** and **Stop processes**. Pausing freezes the whole terminal until you resume it; stopping ends its builds and loops but keeps the agent and its conversation. Also `midna system`, `midna system pause|resume|stop <terminal>`.
- **No more immortal poll loops.** Background loops agents leave polling (`until ! pgrep …; do sleep 5; done`) are stopped after 3 hours (`guard.loop_max_hours`).
- **Idle git worktrees clean themselves up.** midna removes agents' leftover git worktrees after 24 hours without use, once no terminal or process is in them and they have no uncommitted changes. Branches stay. `midna worktrees` shows why each is kept; `midna worktrees clean` runs it now.
- **Closing a terminal cleans up after it.** When an agent terminal closes, a cheap model (Haiku by default) removes what it left behind: the git worktree it worked in, the branches it made and their remote branches. It only removes merged work with nothing uncommitted, never forces anything, never touches `main`, `master`, `develop`, `release/*` or your default branch, and a notification says what it removed and what it kept and why. Add your own items in plain words (`midna cleanup add "stop the docker stack started here"`), turn built-ins off, or skip it once with `midna close <id> --no-cleanup`. **Settings › Agents › Clean up after a terminal closes**; `midna cleanup runs` shows what it did.
- **Notifications that arrive together all reach the list.** When a new notification replaces the one beside the floating badge, the earlier one goes into the badge's list for five times as long, its timer still draining. New ones go straight into a list you have open without moving what you're reading, and a **↑ N new** chip takes you up to them.
- **Pick dates and times instead of typing them.** The schedule editor's times, start and end open a calendar and hour and minute grids under the line; its counts are number fields with − and +, held to their range. In Settings, number rows take digits only, stay inside their limits (the webhook port from 1024 to 65535) and save as you type, and so do notification text and kind names: no more Save buttons.
- **Zoom and pan images with the trackpad.** In the image sheet, pinch or <kbd>⌘</kbd>-scroll to zoom about the pointer, scroll with two fingers to move the image any way (a little past its edges), and <kbd>⌥</kbd>-drag or middle-drag to grab it. Zooming out stops at half the fit.
- **Jump to the terminal an agent mentions.** When terminal text names another terminal by its id (`tab (019da528)`), by an agent's conversation id, or with a `midna://session/<id>` link, <kbd>⌘</kbd>-click it to bring that terminal to the front. Agents are told to name terminals this way. `session.link_at` returns it as `kind: "session"`.
- **Like notification buttons fold together.** On the floating badge's card, buttons that share a first word (Snooze 15 min, Snooze 1 hour) become one split button: the first is one click and the arrow lists the rest. Anything that still doesn't fit goes under **More**, so no button gets cut off. Senders can group buttons themselves with `Later: 1 hour`, `Later: tomorrow`. Notifications take up to 6 buttons of 24 characters each.
- **Settings reads at a glance.** Every row in Settings has a short line of its own under its name instead of the long description written for agents; hover it for the full text. Shortcut names are short, long notes on status rows are trimmed, and the per-kind rows under a heading just name their kind.
- **Settings knows when the daemon is behind.** When the app has updated but its daemon hasn't, Settings marks what the daemon doesn't have yet, says so when a change fails because of it, and **Settings › System › Daemon version** has an **Update daemon** button that upgrades it in place without closing your terminals.
- **Pick and order list settings in place.** In **Settings › Appearance**, the footer stats and buttons, header buttons, status bar items and the row and status scripts are chips: click one to turn it on or off, drag one to move it, and it saves at once. Your own script paths show as chips too; adding a new one is still `midna settings set`.
- **Plan usage and prompt cache in the status bar.** Small 5-hour and weekly bars show how much of your Claude plan is used (amber from 75%, red from 90%); hover one to see when you'll run out at this pace, or about where you'll land by the reset. A flame shows while the selected Claude terminal's prompt cache is still warm, a snowflake once it has gone cold. Right-click the bar to show or hide items, and drag them in that menu to reorder the bar. The policy item is now hidden by default.
- **App links.** Links like `taskboard://#/?task=T6` are clickable when an app on your Mac opens their scheme: ⌘-click opens them in that app, the preview says which app, and they show in the terminal's links. Schemes no app opens stay plain text. `session.link_at` names the app in `app`.
- **Claude plan usage for tools.** `midna usage` (and `usage.get`, `session.get` `agent_info.rate_limits`) shows the 5-hour and weekly limits Claude Code reports, and a `usage.limit_reached` event fires when one runs out. `midna links --kind file --turn last` lists the files an agent edited since your last prompt.
- **Dictation with Kass.** midna opens a real text field as soon as Kass starts listening, so dictation can read and edit what you say in a terminal.
- **Triggers answer a blocked prompt on their own.** A trigger on `agent.prompt_blocked` (like auto-compact: `/compact`, then the prompt again) used to wait forever, because Claude puts the refused prompt back in its input box. midna now clears the box when it holds exactly that prompt and sends the steps; anything else you typed there still makes them wait.
- **Signed updates.** midna checks for updates every few hours, verifies them, and installs them on restart without closing your terminals. Turn on the beta channel in **Settings › Updates** to get new features early.
- **Your own agent arguments.** `midna open --agent claude --resume <id> -- --append-system-prompt "…" --settings board.json` (and `session.open {agent_args, resume}`) start an agent with its own flags and conversation. A `--settings` or `--append-system-prompt` is merged with midna's own, so midna's hooks keep working.
- **Unattended closes.** Turn on **Settings › Agents may force-close terminals** to let an agent close working terminals and use `close --force` without asking you. Off by default.
- **Approvals clean up after themselves.** An approval about a terminal that has since closed, or from an agent that went away, leaves Needs you on its own.
- **An agent that moves on stops needing you.** When an agent raised `midna attention` and then gets a new prompt (your answer, or a message from a tool or another agent), its request leaves Needs you. If it's still stuck, it asks again.
- **Folder trust in Needs you.** When a new Claude asks "Do you trust the files in this folder?", its terminal shows as needs you and you can answer from Needs you.
- **Why a terminal ended.** `session.closed` says how a terminal was closed (close, force close, replace, close on exit, …), a new `agent.session_ended` event records the agent's own reason and who ended it (you, an agent, a restart, or the agent itself), and `session.exited` names the exit (`state`, `reason`). An agent's last hooks no longer fail with "no session" after its terminal is closed.
- **Terminals that close themselves.** `midna open --close-on-exit` (`session.open {close_on_exit: true}`) closes an agent's or command's terminal when it exits normally. A failed exit stays open so you can see why.
- **Unattended updates.** Turn on **Settings › Agents may install updates** (`agents.may_install_updates`) to let an agent run `midna updates install` without asking you, e.g. one that tests each beta as it lands. Signature checks still apply. Off by default.
- **CLI.** `midna get <id>` shows one terminal; `--no-wait` returns a needs-you id instead of waiting for your answer (`midna needs get|wait <id>` follows it); agents may remove a project midna created for their `open --cwd` once its terminals are closed.
- **Closing a terminal moves.** <kbd>⌘W</kbd> folds the closed row away while the rows below slide up, the highlight moves to the terminal shown next, and the pane slides to it the same way. Opening one (<kbd>⌘T</kbd> and the like) runs it in reverse: the new row unfolds, the highlight slides to it and the pane slides it in. Reduce Motion turns it off.
- **Replace a terminal with <kbd>⌘R</kbd>.** It swaps the selected terminal for a fresh one of the same kind in the same project, directory and sidebar slot, then closes the old one: <kbd>⌘T</kbd> + <kbd>⌘W</kbd> without the shuffle. Also in the ⋯ menu, the command bar, and `midna replace <id>` (`session.replace`).
- **Pin a project to the sidebar.** A pinned project stays in the sidebar after its last terminal closes, with a "New terminal" row under its heading; clicking it (or its <kbd>⌘1</kbd>–<kbd>9</kbd>) opens a shell there. Pin from the project's … menu, `midna projects pin|unpin <id>`, or `project.update {pinned}`.
- **<kbd>⌘T</kbd> opens an agent.** <kbd>⌘T</kbd> starts a new terminal running the agent you last started in that project (Claude, Codex…), and <kbd>⇧⌘T</kbd> opens a plain terminal. At root it's <kbd>⌥⌘T</kbd> and <kbd>⌥⇧⌘T</kbd>. Rebind them in **Settings › Shortcuts**.
- **Move between terminals with <kbd>⌥⌘↑</kbd> / <kbd>⌥⌘↓</kbd>.** They select the previous or next terminal in the sidebar, wrapping around, and the highlight and the pane slide to it. Jumping between the prompts you sent an agent no longer has keys by default (bind them in **Settings › Shortcuts**); its ‹ › buttons still work.
- **<kbd>⌘A</kbd> in an agent's input selects your prompt.** In Claude Code's or Codex's input box, <kbd>⌘A</kbd> selects just the text you've typed and moves the cursor to its end, so typing or <kbd>⌫</kbd> replaces it. Elsewhere it still selects the whole screen.
- **<kbd>⌘X</kbd> cuts in the input line.** Select text in Claude Code's or Codex's input box or at a shell prompt (with the mouse, <kbd>⇧</kbd> arrows or <kbd>⌘A</kbd>) and <kbd>⌘X</kbd> copies it and erases it. <kbd>⌘C</kbd> now copies a selection made with the keyboard too.
- **<kbd>⌫</kbd> deletes a mouse selection across rows.** Drag over several lines of an agent's input (or a wrapped shell command) and <kbd>⌫</kbd>, <kbd>⌦</kbd> or typing replaces it, as it already did for a selection on the cursor's row. The cursor walks to the selection's edge first.
- **The sidebar folds away.** <kbd>⌘B</kbd> wipes each line back to its status dot, top to bottom, then closes the panel to the rail, where the dots pop in. Expanding opens the panel first, then writes the lines back in. The terminal resizes once, not on every frame. Reduce Motion turns it off.
- **Schedules that run every 55 minutes.** `--cron '@every 55m'` (also `1h30m`, `5h`) runs on a fixed interval cron can't express: `*/55` means minutes 0 and 55, not every 55 minutes. The first run is one interval after you add it (or at `--starts`), then it keeps that pace. The schedule editor picks it automatically when the number doesn't divide an hour or a day, and no longer describes `*/55` as "Every 55 min".
- **A night asleep isn't idle time.** Idle triggers, `idle_for` queued messages, needs-you expiry and an agent's restart grace count only the time your Mac was awake, so waking it no longer fires them all at once or withdraws requests before you see them. Tools can read midna's sleep log with `clock.sleeps`.
- **Setting requests show the setting.** When an agent asks to change a setting only you can change, Needs you shows it the way Settings does: its name, where it lives, and the value it would get, with what the request adds and removes marked. Change it before you press **Save** (chips, switches and choices are clickable; **Show current** shows what it is now), and the agent can read back what you saved. Notifications say "Change “Status bar items”" and what changes, not the raw command.
